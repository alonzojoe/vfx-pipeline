This is revenue. And this is profit. But what's the difference? Revenue is the total money a business earns from selling products or services. Profit is what's left after subtracting expenses. For example, if a business earns $10,000 and spends $7,000, its profit is $3,000. Now, what about assets and liabilities? Assets are valuable things a business owns, like cash, buildings, and equipment. Liabilities are what a business owes, like loans, unpaid bills, and debts. Revenue is earned. Profit is what's left.
